## Thesis

Baker, Gearhart, and Herman (CSE Technical Report 353, January 1993) is
the primary UCLA evaluation of the Apple Classrooms of Tomorrow (ACOT)
program — the 1985 Apple-funded initiative that put an Apple computer at
both school and home for students and teachers at five sites (four
elementary, one secondary), for study of what happens "when tomorrow's
resources are routinely available in classrooms." The report summarises
1987–1990 findings.

The authors' evaluative posture is triangulation-based: standard
norm-referenced measures (Iowa Tests, published writing rubrics, normed
attitude instruments) alongside newly-developed measures for outcomes
the standard measures could not detect (portfolio composition,
hypermedia knowledge representation, classroom observation). The
underlying premise is that ACOT's ambitious goals — student initiative,
long-term projects, cooperative learning, "instructional guidance
rather than stand-up teaching" — are only partially visible to
traditional measurement.

The core finding is genuinely mixed and heavily qualified. On standard
measures, ACOT students "at least maintained" their performance and
attitudes; on writing, a domain-specific positive effect appeared at
three of five sites; on other academic domains, impact was "negligible
or nonexistent." The authors *positively* value the maintenance of
standard-measure performance as evidence that ACOT's ambition-shift
did not cost basic skills. The authors are explicit that firm inference
is precluded by small sample sizes, comparison-classroom recruitment
problems, and year-to-year site changes. On teachers and instruction,
ACOT teachers' self-reports indicate "fairly typical" practice with
"occasional experimentation" — and, at one site with comparison
teachers, ACOT teachers were *less* likely than comparisons to believe
that computer use helps students grasp concepts or encourages thinking.
Technology-supported innovation was traced to teachers with both
technology skills *and* subject matter / pedagogical expertise, not to
technology access alone.

This is the primary source that Cuban 2021 was summarising with the
compressed phrase "did not erode or much enhance the status quo." The
primary is more textured than the compression: Baker et al. call the
maintenance positive (Cuban's phrasing reads neutral-to-negative), and
Baker et al. specifically document (a) domain-specific writing gains
at three of five sites, (b) a teacher-expertise-mediates-tool
finding, and (c) that instructional goals shaped the type of
technology use (basic skills → drill; enrichment → problem-solving;
authentic-engagement → applications). None of these three is captured
in the Cuban compression, and each bears on a live thread claim.

## Key quoted claims

- "The Apple Classrooms of Tomorrowsm (ACOTsm) project was initiated
  in classrooms at five school sites in 1985 as a program of research
  on the impact of interactive technologies on teaching and
  learning." — p. 1 (locator: text page 1; PDF page 4).
- "ACOT has encouraged instructional innovation, emphasizing to
  participating teachers the potential of computers to support
  student initiative, long-term projects, access to multiple
  resources, cooperative learning, and instructional guidance rather
  than stand-up teaching." — p. 2 (PDF page 5). *The pedagogical
  aim ACOT was pursuing.*
- "Firm conclusions about the effects of ACOT on student and other
  outcomes are constrained by a number of factors: ACOT was
  implemented in a relatively small number of classrooms, often only
  one at a particular grade level, dispersed over an intentionally
  diverse set of school sites; thus ACOT effects were confounded by
  district requirements, teachers' influences, curriculum selection,
  school ambiance, and the characteristics of students, among other
  factors." — pp. 6–7 (PDF pages 9–10). *The methodological caveat.*
- "In general, the results suggested that ACOT students had at least
  maintained their performance levels on standard measures of
  educational achievement in basic skills and had sustained positive
  attitudes." — p. 7 (PDF page 10). *The primary the Cuban 2021
  paraphrase "did not erode ... the status quo" compresses.*
- "Generally, the ACOT program appeared as effective in promoting
  commonly measured student outcomes as the more typical
  instructional programs provided by the comparison sites." — p. 7
  (PDF page 10).
- "There were indications that ACOT enhanced students' writing
  abilities at one site in 1988-89, and three in 1989-90 (for a
  total of three sites across the two years). Impact in other
  domains of student achievement was negligible or nonexistent." —
  p. 7 (PDF page 10). *The domain-specific effect Cuban 2021's
  compression drops.*
- "We viewed the overall maintenance of the status quo positively." —
  p. 8 (PDF page 11). *Baker et al. positively evaluate the very
  finding Cuban's compression leaves neutral.*
- "ACOT teachers' reports of their classroom practices indicated
  fairly typical subject matter emphases and pedagogical methods,
  along with occasional experimentation with the use of technology
  to support innovative practices such as cooperative group work or
  long-term student projects." — p. 9 (PDF page 12). *The finding
  most inconvenient for a "give teachers computers → ambitious
  learning" story.*
- "ACOT teachers were less likely than comparison teachers to
  believe that computer use helps students grasp concepts easily or
  encourages students to think more." — p. 10 (PDF page 13). *A
  direct empirical tension for tech-optimist framings.*
- "technology-supported innovation and experimentation appeared more
  likely among teachers who had not only technology skills but
  also—and quite critically—subject matter knowledge, curriculum
  knowledge (understandings of new methods of teaching a particular
  subject area), and competence with nontraditional pedagogy (e.g.,
  cooperative grouping, student collaboration, long-term projects).
  Sound educational practice requires well-informed, solidly grounded
  rationale." — p. 13 (PDF page 16). *Direct primary-source
  support for a teacher-agency-thread claim: teacher expertise is
  the mediating variable, not the tool.*
- "A focus on improving basic skills led to use of computer-based
  instructional software; an interest in enrichment resulted in
  'problem-solving' software or student choice of software; a focus
  on use of computer resources for authentic engagement in
  productive work required applications such as word processors,
  graphics programs, databases, and spreadsheets." — pp. 12–13 (PDF
  pages 15–16). *Empirical: instructional aim shapes technology
  use, not the reverse. Direct support for the 2026-05-27 steer's
  "method-agnostic in service of the aim" claim, on ACOT data.*
- "Certainly the core measures do not reflect well many of ACOT's
  goals. But what do they reflect, and are the competences assessed
  by traditional measures important outcomes for any school program?" —
  p. 13 (PDF page 16). *The measurement-adequacy caveat that
  underlies the whole "inconclusive" character of ACOT results.*

## Methods

Multi-site, multi-year (1987–1990), triangulation-based formative
evaluation of five ACOT sites. Data types: norm-referenced achievement
tests (Iowa Tests of Basic Skills subtests), writing prompts scored on
IEA-derived scales, normed attitude measures (SAM, SCAMIN),
UCLA-designed teacher and parent questionnaires (spring 1989, spring
1990), and a quantitative classroom observation instrument developed
in 1988–89 and deployed at two sites. Comparison classrooms were
identified fall 1988 for most sites, but participation of comparison
classrooms was "generally problematic" and comparison-teacher
survey response was weak or absent, which the authors flag as a
significant constraint on inference. Design compromises across years
(reduced testing burden per site, site-specific writing prompt
selection, revised measures) further constrained longitudinal and
cross-site comparison. Small numbers per grade level per site
compound the inference problem: often one classroom per grade level
per site, five diverse sites.

## Where this sits in the canon

Primary empirical source underlying the tech-cycles thread's ACOT case
that was previously on the ledger only as a Cuban 2021 paraphrase. The
Cuban 2021 → Baker 1993 upgrade means the ACOT entry now has (a) a
directly-quotable primary-source finding, (b) two additional
substantive Baker-1993 findings not captured in the Cuban paraphrase
(the teacher-expertise-mediates finding; the aim-shapes-tool
finding), and (c) an evaluator-side positive framing of the
"maintenance of the status quo" result that the Cuban compression
loses. Also relevant to the augmentation-not-efficiency thread (the
aim-shapes-tool finding is direct primary support for the
2026-05-27 steer's "method-agnostic in service of the aim"
formulation) and to the teacher-agency thread (the
teacher-expertise-mediates finding is direct primary support for the
workload-as-condition, teacher-as-not-bottleneck reframing).

Historically situated in the same 1985–1995 ambitious-1:1-deployment
period Watters 2019 characterises as an "utter failure" and Cuban 2021
characterises as "did not erode or much enhance the status quo." Both
prior secondary characterisations are more definitive than the primary
supports: Baker et al. are careful about what the data can and cannot
say, and the authors' explicit positive read of the "maintenance"
finding is at odds with both prior secondary readings.

## Tensions / contradictions

- **With Cuban 2021.** Cuban's compressed characterisation loses the
  writing-specific gain (3 of 5 sites) and reframes Baker's
  positively-valued "maintenance" as neutral-to-negative. The
  compression is defensible for a short-form blog post but should not
  be relied on when the underlying claim matters for a thread.
- **With Watters 2019.** Watters 2019's "utter failure" reading of the
  1980s-90s ambitious deployments is more definitive than Baker et al.
  themselves. The primary is genuinely inconclusive with domain-
  specific positive findings — not a failure.
- **Internal to Baker 1993.** ACOT teachers were *less* likely than
  comparison teachers to believe that computer use helps concepts /
  encourages thinking — a striking piece of evidence that the
  ambitious-deployment cohort was, on some dimensions, more sceptical
  than the comparison cohort. The report does not resolve whether
  this reflects better-informed scepticism (ACOT teachers had more
  exposure to computer use, so their assessment carries different
  weight) or an experience-souring effect. Either reading complicates
  the tech-optimist framing.
- **With the augmentation-thread positive case.** The Baker 1993
  aim-shapes-tool finding *supports* the 2026-05-27 steer's
  "method-agnostic in service of the aim" claim, but it does so in a
  way that also complicates it: at ACOT, "an interest in enrichment
  resulted in 'problem-solving' software" — i.e. the aim shaped
  the tool, but the deployed tool at that time was itself
  method-carrying (drill software → drill; problem-solving software
  → problem-solving). The 2026 AI case is not fully analogous
  because LLM tools are less method-carrying than drill/problem-
  solving software was in 1990; but the mechanism (aim → tool
  choice → pedagogy) is directly relevant and the ambient tool
  ecology at the deployment moment matters.
