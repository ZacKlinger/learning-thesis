## Thesis

Watters' end-of-decade summary. Ten prior year-end series compressed into
a numbered list from 100 (Horizon Report) down to 1 (anti-school-shooter
software). It is structurally a chronicle, not an argument — but the
ranking encodes the argument. The top of the list is what she thinks
mattered most as a *pattern*, not what was most reported: #3 Venture
Capitalism, #2 (Venture) Philanthropy, #1 Anti-School Shooter Software.
The two-word items at #2 and #3 do almost no descriptive work; they
identify the *structural interests* she thinks shaped the decade.

For the tech-cycles thread the piece is important less for what it says
than for what it does *not* say. There is no restatement of the 2018
piece's analytical frame ("augment, not replace, of course"). The 2019
mode is chronicle-and-anger rather than argument; the ten years of hype
are recorded as accumulated failure. Two implications for the steer's
setup-not-foil reframing: (a) the historical-continuity move that the
setup framing depends on (this wave is the latest in a long series) is
explicitly present here — the "learn to code" entry pulls the myth back
to Sputnik 1957 — but (b) the *use* Watters puts it to is foil-shaped,
not setup-shaped. She is not saying "the medium of education has been
changing all along"; she is saying "the myth has been recycling all
along, and this decade proves it kept on failing." The tech-cycles
thread's setup-not-foil reframing has to answer Watters' 2019 piece,
not just her 2018 one.

For the augmentation thread the piece is a large practitioner-scale
case-book of what happened when the "personalized learning" version of
tech-in-education scaled up. Summit / CZI / Basecamp, AltSchool, Knewton,
Bridge International, ClassDojo, Rocketship — all documented as
concrete cases. Watters would say the entire decade's evidence tells
against the augmentation aim; the ledger should say more carefully that
she is documenting the *efficiency-and-surveillance* half of the
augmentation-vs-efficiency binary and that the S&B 1994 knowledge-
building tradition is largely absent from the Watters ledger. That
absence is itself a datum.

For the teacher-agency thread the Bridge International Academies entry
(#18) is the sharpest instance in the piece of the teacher-as-conduit
pattern the thread engages: scripted lessons delivered to teachers via
mobile device, teacher attendance tracked via mobile device, teacher
pay disbursed via mobile device. This is a K-12-adjacent (Global South)
concrete case for what happens when workload-relief is delivered as
substitution rather than augmentation.

The piece has no new *empirical* claims for the ledger — it is a review,
not a study. What it adds is (i) a batch of contemporary practitioner-
scale cases; (ii) a historical-continuity locator (Sputnik 1957); and
(iii) a stronger political-economy critique than the 2018 piece — the
venture capital and venture philanthropy that fund the frame Watters
2018 called out are here ranked as #3 and #2 debacles of the decade in
their own right.

## Where this sits in the canon

- The direct sequel to Watters 2018 on the ledger. 2018 was analytical
  (why "augment, not replace" is the wrong frame; the algorithmic-
  prediction critique). 2019 is enumerative (the decade in 100 cases).
  The tone shift is real. The 2018 piece read as a warning; the 2019
  piece reads as a summary judgment.
- The tech-cycles thread's fourth Watters/Cuban entry after Watters
  2018, Cuban 2024, Cuban 2025.
- Complementary to Cuban 2024/2025: Cuban writes from a
  practitioner-historian frame concerned with what actually happens
  inside classrooms; Watters writes from a political-economy frame
  concerned with who funds and shapes the ed-tech sector. Both are
  useful; they cut at different levels of the unit-of-analysis
  question the 2026-06-20 digest surfaced.
- Sits in the same lineage as her forthcoming (at time of publication)
  book *Teaching Machines* (2021) — the historical continuity move
  from Sputnik 1957 (and elsewhere in the piece, back to the 1920s)
  is a preview of the book's argument.

## Tensions / contradictions

- **vs. the 2026-05-27 steer's setup-not-foil reframing of tech-cycles.**
  The steer asks: read historical waves as "the medium of learning has
  been changing all along, AI is the next change" (setup), not as
  "every prior wave failed so this one has to prove itself" (foil).
  Watters 2019 is not neutral on this. Her decade-in-review is
  explicitly *foil-shaped*: the pattern of hype-and-failure is the
  point. She does the historical-continuity move (Sputnik 1957) but
  puts it to a use the steer rejects. The thread needs to distinguish
  between two claims that Watters conflates: (a) the *technology* keeps
  changing (setup); (b) the *hype-and-failure cycle* keeps repeating
  (foil). Setup-not-foil is defensible; but the essay must engage
  Watters 2019 directly rather than borrowing her historical-continuity
  move while rejecting her use of it.
- **vs. the augmentation-not-efficiency thread's positive case.** The
  entire ledger of practitioner-scale cases in Watters 2019 sits on
  the *efficiency-and-surveillance* side of the augmentation-vs-
  efficiency binary. The Scardamalia & Bereiter tradition of knowledge-
  building communities is absent from Watters' review. Two possible
  reads: (i) the S&B tradition failed to scale into the practitioner
  landscape Watters is describing (a data-point *against* the
  augmentation aim's practitioner viability); (ii) the S&B tradition
  is not what venture-capital-funded ed-tech has been trying to build
  (a data-point that the venture-capital-funded ed-tech that failed is
  the *wrong* corpus to judge the augmentation aim by). Both are true;
  they cut differently on the essay's argument.
- **vs. Cuban 2025's bottom-up-adoption datum.** Cuban 2025 recorded
  that some teachers are adopting AI in ways that suggest a different
  adoption pathway from the top-down district-purchase pattern that
  dominated the 2010s. Watters 2019 does not engage this possibility
  directly (the AI moment postdates her piece by three years). But
  the ClassDojo entry (#7) and the Summit / CZI / AltSchool entries
  are all cases where teacher-level or student-level adoption was
  *reported* but the actual purchasing and design decisions were made
  well above the classroom. The Cuban 2025 read is that AI's early
  adoption pattern may differ from these; Watters 2019 would want
  strong evidence before conceding that.
- **The absence of an "augment, not replace" restatement.** The 2018
  piece had one. The 2019 piece does not. This is either (a) a mode
  shift (chronicle vs. essay), or (b) an evolution (Watters no longer
  thinks the analytical frame is worth restating because the decade
  has settled the question), or (c) both. The augmentation thread's
  "rhetorical-proximity warning" recorded from Watters 2018 remains
  active; the 2019 piece neither strengthens nor weakens it directly,
  but the ranking of #2 and #3 (philanthropy and venture capital)
  sharpens the political-economy backing for the warning.
