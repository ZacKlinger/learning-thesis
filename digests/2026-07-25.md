## What I read this week

- [Cuban 2021 — "Whatever Happened to Apple Classroom of Tomorrow?"](../sources/Cuban-2021-acot-apple-classroom.md) —
  a compact retrospective on ACOT, the Apple-funded 1985–1995
  experiment that put 1:1 desktop computers in selected classrooms.
  The load-bearing content is a Jane David 1986 field report that
  Cuban excerpts, ending with David's list of five "ingrained
  characteristics of the existing system" that constrained the
  innovation. Cuban's own diagnostic move is to name that same list
  as "a pointed reminder that efforts to integrate computers into
  daily lessons must reckon with these questions and imperatives in
  2021." The Baker et al. 1993 UCLA evaluation Cuban summarises
  found that 1:1 computers "did not erode or much enhance the
  status quo" — a methodologically inconclusive result, not a
  clear failure.

- [Cuban 2022 — "Whatever Happened to Programmed Learning?"](../sources/Cuban-2022-programmed-learning.md) —
  a compact history of Skinner-tradition programmed instruction
  from Pressey (1928) through the 1950s–60s hype peak through its
  1970s disappearance from reform rhetoric, its 1990s resurrection
  as "computer-assisted instruction," and its present-day survival
  in drill software and (Cuban's specific claim) in the ubiquitous
  classroom worksheet — though at degraded fidelity because
  worksheets typically lack the immediate-feedback element that was
  the theoretical core. Cuban's mode is diagnostic, not
  condemnatory: the tradition did not fail, it was absorbed,
  renamed, and preserved at lower fidelity across seven decades.

## Tensions I noticed

The primary tension this week is one Cuban 2022 sharpens for the
augmentation thread specifically. The steer's clarified thesis is
that AI removes four constraints so that "the truest forms of
learning, whatever the research says those forms actually are"
become available. The specific worry Cuban 2022 documents is that
the Pressey/Skinner tradition of atomised-steps-with-feedback has
survived every prior wave of educational technology through
relabelling — and that the closest short-form description of what
a modal LLM-tutor deployment does is *exactly* the Pressey/Skinner
workflow with the immediate-feedback element finally intact
(feedback was the piece the worksheet form had lost). On this
reading, the default AI-tutor is not the augmentation aim in
disguise; it is programmed learning finally with feedback again.

This is not a refutation of the augmentation aim — the aim can be
defended against this challenge, and the Scardamalia & Bereiter
1994 knowledge-building framework is the closest existing answer
on the ledger. The challenge is that the augmentation aim is *not
what AI-tutors do by default*. It requires a specific design choice
to realise it — the LLM-as-knowledge-base-collaborator design, not
the LLM-as-drill-partner design. The essay owes a specification of
what makes an augmentation-aim AI deployment structurally different
from a programmed-learning-at-fidelity AI deployment. That
specification cannot live at the "AI helps learners" abstraction
level; it has to live at the design-pattern level.

A smaller but related tension: Cuban 2021's ACOT case and Cuban
2022's programmed-learning case document two structurally different
failure modes for ambitious ed-tech. ACOT (hardware-first,
instructional-model-agnostic) failed because the surrounding
school conditions absorbed the innovation. Programmed learning
(instructional-model-first, hardware-agnostic) "failed" — Cuban
would say metabolised — because the theory was preserved in
practice at lower fidelity. The augmentation aim's positive case
has to be design-first (both the model and the surrounding
conditions specified together). If the essay commits to being
design-first, the two Cuban 2021/2022 failure-mode catalogues become
material the design specification has to route around. That's a
useful positioning constraint the thread did not have before this
Saturday.

## Thesis-fit check

Operative direction (2026-05-27 steer): *"AI is method-agnostic in
service of the aim... The optimistic case is not about a particular
pedagogy. It is about removing the constraints that have forced
education to compromise on its aim."*

**This week's reading complicates this line without falsifying it.**
Cuban 2022's specification challenge — that the modal AI-tutor
deployment is programmed learning at higher fidelity — is a
challenge to the "method-agnostic" claim. It suggests that AI *is*
being method-shaped by the default design pattern LLM chatbots
land in when deployed at scale. This is not a claim that a
method-agnostic AI deployment is impossible; it is a claim that
the *modal* deployment will be method-specific (Pressey/Skinner-
descendant) unless someone makes a specific design choice to make
it otherwise.

The steer's response — the aim is not about a particular pedagogy
— is defensible in principle but has an operational cost the
thread should acknowledge. Being method-agnostic *in principle*
does not remove the risk that AI in practice defaults to a
specific method (the Pressey/Skinner-descendant drill workflow),
and the essay owes an account of what specifically the aim
requires *by way of design constraints* on AI deployments if it
is not to default to the modal pattern.

Cuban 2021 puts a related but different constraint on the steer.
The four constraints frame is technology-side (what removes cost /
one-way content / workload / gap to expert practice). David 1986's
five-item list of "ingrained characteristics of the existing
system" is a school-side list of what constrains any ambitious
deployment. The two lists do not overlap much — David's constraints
(teacher-centered instruction, district curricular objectives,
misaligned standardised tests, grade-level coordination pressure,
next-grade preparation) are the coordination-and-evaluation regime
that ACOT could not change. AI's removal of constraint #1 (cost of
expert attention) does not touch any of David's five. This does
not mean the steer's four constraints are wrong; it means the
constraints Cuban/David identify are additional to the steer's,
and the thesis should say so.

**A recommendation for you to consider.** Two candidate refinements
of the steer's framing, either of which would engage both this
week's readings:

*(a)* Keep the four constraints as the *technology-side* claim,
and add a *conditions-side* claim: an ambitious AI deployment
requires the four technology-side constraints to be removed *and*
some subset of the school-side constraints David identifies to be
loosened. The essay would then have to specify which subset — a
narrower version of the augmentation aim, but a more defensible
one against the Cuban 2021 counter-case.

*(b)* Keep the "method-agnostic aim" language but add a design-
constraint claim: the aim is method-agnostic in principle but
requires specific design choices at deployment (LLM-as-knowledge-
base-collaborator, not LLM-as-drill-partner; S&B-tradition designs,
not Pressey-tradition-with-feedback designs). The essay would then
have to specify the design choices — the augmentation aim would be
about a class of designs, not a class of technologies.

Both are defensible; they are not mutually exclusive; and the
choice between them is above the agent's pay grade. Neither is
"the synthesis" the steer scheduled — but the synthesis will have
to choose between them or find a third route.
