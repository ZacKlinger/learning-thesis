## What I read this week

- [Baker, Gearhart & Herman 1993 — "The Apple Classrooms of Tomorrow: The UCLA Evaluation Studies" (CSE Technical Report 353)](../sources/Baker-Gearhart-Herman-1993-acot-ucla-evaluation.md) —
  the primary formative-evaluation report on ACOT from UCLA's Center
  for the Study of Evaluation, summarising 1987–1990 findings across
  five ACOT sites. The core finding: ACOT students "at least
  maintained" performance on standard measures and sustained positive
  attitudes; writing showed a domain-specific gain at three of five
  sites; effects in other academic domains were "negligible or
  nonexistent"; the authors *positively value* the maintenance-of-
  status-quo finding as evidence that the ambition-shift did not
  cost basic skills. Two Baker-specific findings not in Cuban 2021:
  (a) teacher subject-matter and pedagogical expertise (not
  technology skills or the tech endowment) mediated whether the
  1:1 deployment produced ambitious pedagogy; (b) ACOT teachers
  were *more* sceptical than comparison teachers about computer
  benefits — either better-informed scepticism or an experience-
  souring effect, unresolvable from Baker 1993 alone. A third
  novel finding: instructional aim shaped software use ("A focus
  on improving basic skills led to use of computer-based
  instructional software; an interest in enrichment resulted in
  'problem-solving' software or student choice of software; a
  focus on use of computer resources for authentic engagement in
  productive work required applications such as word processors,
  graphics programs, databases, and spreadsheets") — direct
  empirical support for the steer's method-agnostic formulation.

## Tensions I noticed

The most interesting tension this week is *inside* Baker 1993 rather
than between Baker and other sources.

ACOT teachers were **less** likely than comparison teachers to believe
that computer use helps students grasp concepts easily or encourages
students to think more. The paired finding is that ACOT teachers
themselves reported "fairly typical" subject-matter emphases and
pedagogical methods with only "occasional experimentation" — despite
the whole point of ACOT being to enable non-typical pedagogy. Two
readings of this pair are compatible with the primary data and neither
is decidable from the report alone:

*(a) Better-informed scepticism.* ACOT teachers had 3+ years of
extensive computer exposure; the comparison teachers had much less.
On this reading, the scepticism is a signal — the tech-optimist
priors were adjusted downward by prolonged contact with actual
classroom outcomes, and the "fairly typical" self-report of practice
reflects an honest assessment that the tools did not enable the
ambitious pedagogy the ACOT program aim required.

*(b) Experience-souring.* ACOT teachers might have started ambitious
and been ground down by the constraints David 1986 catalogued
(grade-level coordination pressure, standardised-test misalignment,
next-grade preparation requirements). On this reading, the scepticism
is a symptom — teachers who tried to do the ambitious thing were
punished by the surrounding coordination-and-evaluation regime and
reverted to "fairly typical" practice.

Reading (a) supports the workload-as-condition reframing directly:
the teachers were exercising judgment, and their judgment was that
ambitious use requires structural conditions the deployment did not
supply. Reading (b) supports the same reframing but with a
sharper edge — even ambitious teachers with the technology in hand
were structurally prevented from realising the ambitious deployment.

Both readings are compatible with — and neither is falsified by —
the 2026-05-27 steer's clarified thesis. But the pair together is a
specific challenge to a naïve "give teachers AI tools and they will
teach ambitiously" version of the augmentation aim: teachers who
had "tomorrow's" tools available at 1:1, at home and at school, over
years, in a well-funded program with explicit ambitious pedagogical
guidance from the program leadership, reported "fairly typical"
practice and were more sceptical of the tools than their peers who
had less exposure. The 2026 AI case is not automatically better
positioned than ACOT was in this respect — LLM tools have different
affordances, but the surrounding coordination-and-evaluation regime
is the same regime David 1986 catalogued (Cuban 2021's "in my opinion,
not only a glimpse into the past but also a pointed reminder... in
2021" claim).

A secondary tension this week is between Baker's aim → tool → pedagogy
mechanism and Cuban 2022's specification challenge on the augmentation
thread. Baker's data show that the aim genuinely did shape the tool
selection at ACOT — enrichment-oriented classrooms adopted problem-
solving software; basic-skills-oriented classrooms adopted drill
software. Cuban 2022's argument is that the modal deployment of a
general-purpose tool (worksheets, then computers) reverts to
Pressey/Skinner-style drill because the tool is not method-carrying
and someone will fill the vacuum. The two are compatible: at ACOT the
aim shaped the tool because the aim was set locally (site leadership,
teacher choice); Cuban 2022 warns that at scale the aim is set by
whoever supplies the modal LLM deployment (ambient product design,
platform defaults). The augmentation thesis's design-position claim
now has to specify *at what unit-of-analysis* the aim gets set — a
version of the unit-of-analysis question the thread has been carrying
since 2026-06-20.

## Thesis-fit check

Operative direction (2026-05-27 steer): *"AI is method-agnostic in
service of the aim... The optimistic case is not about a particular
pedagogy. It is about removing the constraints that have forced
education to compromise on its aim."*

**This week's reading strengthens the "method-agnostic in service of
the aim" line and, at the same time, sharpens what "in service of
the aim" would have to mean operationally.**

Baker 1993 is a direct empirical demonstration that the aim shapes
the tool choice in an ambitious ed-tech deployment. That is a
positive primary-source datum for the steer's formulation — the
ledger previously had this claim only through the 2026-05-27 steer's
own framing and the Cuban 2025 teacher-voice data. Now it has the
mechanism attested in a peer-review-adjacent empirical evaluation.

Baker 1993 also sharpens what the "aim" is. At ACOT, the aim was set
at the classroom level by individual teachers with subject-matter
and pedagogical expertise. The mediating variable was teacher
judgment. Whether the same mechanism will operate for AI in 2026
depends on whether teachers have — and are supported in exercising —
the same kind of judgment. This is a specific version of the
Cuban/David "structural conditions" concern: the aim → tool → pedagogy
mechanism operates through teacher judgment, and teacher judgment
operates within the coordination-and-evaluation regime the school and
district set. Removing constraint #1 (cost of expert attention) does
not automatically put the aim in the teacher's hands; it depends on
whether the surrounding regime lets the teacher's aim be operative.

A recommendation for you to consider. **The synthesis Zack promised
to draft (or authorise the agent to draft) should now specify at
what unit the aim gets set.** Three candidates from the ledger:

*(1) Teacher-level aim.* The Baker 1993 case: individual teachers
with expertise set the aim; the tool shapes to it. Vulnerable to
Cuban 2022's warning that at scale the modal deployment overrides
local aim-setting.

*(2) School-level aim.* The Bryk 2010 case: essential supports
combine to establish a coherent school-level orientation, inside
which teacher judgment operates. Compatible with the workload-as-
condition reframing; requires a specific answer to how the school-
level aim gets set (charter, network, principal, teachers-in-council).

*(3) Platform-level aim.* The Cuban 2022 warning: the ambient LLM
deployment sets the modal aim, and teachers/schools work with what
the platform gives them. Least optimistic reading; requires the
essay to argue that the platform aim will be set well, which is a
much harder claim.

The steer's four-constraint frame is compatible with any of these,
but the essay needs to pick one (or specify how they combine).
Without that, the augmentation aim is a target without a mechanism.
