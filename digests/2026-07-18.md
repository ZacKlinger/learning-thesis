## Tensions I noticed

The primary tension this week is one Watters 2019 sharpens: the
2026-05-27 steer's setup-not-foil reframing of the tech-cycles
thread has a specific vulnerability that Watters 2018 already put
pressure on, and that Watters 2019 hardens.

The setup framing says: the medium of learning has been changing
throughout history; AI is the latest, most consequential change.
Read the historical record as continuous evolution — new medium,
new possibility.

Watters 2019 also uses the historical record — she pulls the
"learn to code" myth back to Sputnik 1957 — but for the opposite
rhetorical purpose. Her use is: the ed-tech-hype pattern has been
recycling for the entire post-war era, and the 2010s are the
latest instance of it failing. "The MOOC revolution simply wasn't."
"What an utter failure." She is not writing about the medium
changing; she is writing about the *funder pattern* recycling.
Ranking Venture Capitalism at #3 and (Venture) Philanthropy at #2
is not a soft-focus critique; it is a specific identification of
where she thinks the recurrent failure comes from.

The tension for the thesis: both claims — the-medium-is-changing
(steer, setup) and the-hype-cycle-recycles (Watters, foil) — are
about the same historical record. They differ on which pattern is
*load-bearing* for explanation. If the steer's setup framing is
right, then the load-bearing pattern is capability evolution and
the funder-pattern is a compounding-but-secondary problem. If
Watters' 2019 framing is right, then the load-bearing pattern is
the funder-pattern and any capability evolution is coloured by who
funds and shapes its deployment.

I do not think Watters is straightforwardly right — the entry's
counter-evidence records why (chronicle is by-construction
selection-biased; the piece pre-dates the ChatGPT wave; Bridge is
a Global South case that doesn't modally represent US K-12). But
I do not think the steer is straightforwardly right either. The
essay owes a specific answer to *what structurally distinguishes
AI's change of medium from the 100 cases in Watters 2019* — an
answer that goes beyond the bottom-up-adoption datum Cuban 2025
supplies, which is a difference in *pathway* (top-down district
purchase vs. bottom-up teacher/student adoption) rather than proof
that the *destination* is different.

A smaller but related tension: Watters' ranking of #2 and #3
(philanthropy and capital) implies that the *funding structure* is
the load-bearing variable. The steer's four constraints (cost of
expert attention; one-way fixed content; system-imposed workload;
gap to expert practice) are partially independent of who funds the
removal — constraint #1, in particular, is removed whether OpenAI
or a university library funds the tutor. But constraints #3 and
#4 are more entangled with who deploys the tools and to what end.
Watters' 2019 hardening is a specific challenge to constraints #3
and #4, less to #1 and #2.

## Thesis-fit check

Operative direction (2026-05-27 steer): *"Tech-cycles is setup, not
foil. The historical wave-by-wave pattern is the frame — the
medium of learning has been changing all along, AI is the next
change. It is not the obstacle — 'every prior wave failed so this
one has to prove itself.' When Cuban / Watters / Postman land,
read them as historical setup for 'the medium evolves,' not as a
base rate the optimistic case has to beat."*

**This week's reading puts pressure on this line without
falsifying it.** Watters 2019 is the third Watters entry on the
ledger (with 2018 and, implicitly, the 2014/2015 pieces still
unread in `sources-raw/`). Across the three Watters entries the
pattern is clear: she does not write the medium-evolves setup
narrative the steer wants; she writes the funder-pattern-recycles
foil narrative the steer rejects. The two are compatible as
descriptions of the same historical record, but they are not
interchangeable as *frames* for what the thesis is trying to argue.

The steer says: read Watters as setup, not foil. I have honestly
tried. What I keep finding is that Watters writes in a foil mode,
and reading her *as* setup requires the reader to do more of the
work than the text supports. The recorded entry on the tech-cycles
thread is careful to distinguish the two claims (the technology-
in-education pattern is one of evolution; the ed-tech-hype-cycle
pattern is one of recurrent failure) and to note that Watters
agrees with the first and rejects the steer's use of it because
she also asserts the second.

**A recommendation for you to consider.** The setup-not-foil
reframing is defensible if — and I think only if — the thesis
takes on the work of specifying what structurally distinguishes
AI's change of medium from the 100 cases in Watters 2019 (and
from the century of cases in Cuban / Watters 2021). "Bottom-up
adoption" is a start, not a finish. Two other candidate
distinguishers to consider: (a) the *generality* of the medium
(prior waves were medium-specific — film, television, tablets;
generative AI is medium-general in a way that the printing press
and the internet were, and those two were the successful cases in
Watters' otherwise-cyclical record); (b) the *cost curve* (the
marginal cost of expert-level reasoning has fallen many orders of
magnitude in a way that has no precedent in prior waves). Neither
is on the ledger yet as an argued claim; both are candidates for
the tech-cycles thread's positive case.
